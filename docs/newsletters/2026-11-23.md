## Breaking News

**Reported: Claude helped certify a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis — a 167-year-old unsolved math problem — via a 17-million-line Lean proof.** According to the AI Revolution roundup ([u5aACewFWaw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u5aACewFWaw)), Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says Claude "outpaced him at the finish line" of the proof, and the Lean and Mathlib communities have since certified it. Your AI Guy's coverage ([lCe7tqNcLeE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lCe7tqNcLeE)) is more cautious, framing the story as "reported" — the right default until a formal write-up circulates. The key detail is *machine-checked*: unlike most LLM math demos, this claim doesn't rest on a chatbot's confident prose but on a proof assistant's step-by-step verification, which is a much stronger category of evidence.

A second, related Claude result surfaced via Two Minute Papers ([1jPdehG3Hms](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1jPdehG3Hms)): an Anthropic/Hexagon Math paper on black-hole physics ([anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics), [hexagonmath.org/2611.00175v1](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00175v1)). If the Riemann work were a one-off you could shrug; if Claude is producing a run of formally verified results, the "AI as research collaborator" story gets much more concrete — but stay skeptical of the "superintelligence is here" framing until the math community weighs in beyond the Lean certification.

Make the most of it:
- **Add formal-verification tasks to your evals.** Lean/Mathlib-style formalization is one of the few reasoning benchmarks where the checker, not a judge or LLM-as-judge, decides correctness. Watch whether open-weight releases start reporting formal-math scores — that's a signal of real reasoning gains rather than benchmark cramming.
- **Borrow the draft-then-verify loop for agents.** The reported workflow — model drafts, tool verifies, human closes the loop — maps directly onto coding agents with test harnesses: generate, verify, and spend your review budget where it matters.
- **Don't re-architect for one demo.** Treat this as a strong signal, not a reason to bet the roadmap on AI mathematicians. Keep your eval harness model-agnostic so you can test the next model release locally on the same tasks.

## Work Watch — Snowflake, dbt & Cortex Code

### Snowflake

**AI_COMPLETE now supports Snowflake Decision (public preview, Nov 20, 2026).** Snowflake's release notes ([1](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/all-release-notes)) add a new purpose-built model, Snowflake Decision, callable through AI_COMPLETE ([6](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2026/other/2026-11-20-ai-complete-snowflake-decision-public-preview)). It targets high-throughput classification, scoring, and decision-making across large datasets: evaluate unstructured data against predefined criteria and get structured answers with probabilities and confidence values — think ticket routing, record prioritization, or lead scoring without leaving SQL. That's the kind of feature that can replace brittle `CASE WHEN` heuristics, though it's worth benchmarking latency and cost per million rows against a small local model before committing any critical pipeline.

**Snowflake app runtime: run Node.js and Python apps directly on Snowflake.** A new Snowflake Developers walkthrough ([rEzpwkQbzik](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rEzpwkQbzik)) shows Snow App Deploy in the Snowflake CLI uploading and building your code, storing app versions, and returning a live URL. The use case that jumps out for a data/AI engineer: internal tools that currently sit in a tiny container somewhere can live inside Snowflake next to the data. Watch for platform lock-in and warehouse-compute billing, but the DX — CLI-driven, versioned deploys — looks genuinely convenient for shipping small internal apps.

dbt and Cortex Code: no news in today's available material.

## Headlines

**Thinking Machines ships Inkling 2 Mini, a free local model that matches Qwen 3.8 27B.** Devsplainers ([dsUStdaUwsc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dsUStdaUwsc)) reports that Mira Murati's lab released Inkling 2 Mini, tying Qwen 3.8 27B on Artificial Analysis at a claimed ~5x speedup, and tests whether it should replace Qwen on your machine. The interesting memory-efficiency trick: 26.4B parameters but only 3.4B active per token, with two middle blocks executed three times — 24 stored layers doing the work of 48. Treat "free local" as distinct from "open-weight" until the license details are confirmed, and check independent coding scores before rebuilding any workflow on it.

**OpenAI publishes a Franklin Templeton investment-research case study.** The OpenAI channel's short film ([gAmjBqXNoFI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gAmjBqXNoFI)) profiles Katrina Dudley explaining how the firm connects compliance, risk, and trading information and turns it into insight for portfolio managers — the thesis being that "just delivering data is not enough." For anyone building LLM-powered decision support, it's a clean example of the pattern: model over your data, guardrailed by compliance data, concluding with a human making the call.

**Simon Willison's Select Styling Specimens.** A small but nice utility from his blog ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/22/select-styling/)): a tool for comparing how HTML `<select>` boxes render across browsers, born from his frustration with default styling.
