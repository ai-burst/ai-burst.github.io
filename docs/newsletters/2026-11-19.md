## Breaking News

A rare three-front model launch day: Google officially introduced **Gemini 4 Neon**, while OpenAI's **GPT-6.2 Astra / Sol** and Alibaba's **Qwen 4 Max** dominate creator coverage.

**Gemini 4 Neon has the strongest paper trail.** Google's announcement is up on the official blog ([Google AI blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/)), and Simon Willison shipped `llm-gemini` 0.38 with CLI support the same day ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/18/llm-gemini/)). His main warning: Gemini 4 pricing was already confusing, and Neon "has somehow made this even worse." For Snowflake users, Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Hydrogen are also already in Public Preview inside Cortex AI ([Snowflake release notes](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/all-release-notes)).

**GPT-6.2 Astra and Qwen 4 Max are currently launch-day YouTube claims, not vendor-doc facts.** Per AI Revolution and AI Search, OpenAI's Astra is claimed to beat Claude Fable 5.2 on major benchmarks at 5% of the cost, while Sol delivers near-flagship performance for far less; the same coverage reports early testers calling it OpenAI's worst model and "troubling" safety-test behavior ([AI Revolution](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_VXBCcECXJ4), [AI Search](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rYsIpPP6QY4)). Your AI Guy's Qwen 4 Max video describes Alibaba's new model as its most powerful yet, with focus on reasoning, coding, and comparisons to OpenAI/Google/Anthropic ([Your AI Guy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q4uB9rlmNA0)). No official OpenAI or Qwen release notes surfaced in this feed, so treat those benchmark and "High AI self-improvement threshold" claims as unverified for now.

**How to make the most of it:**

1. If you use Gemini from the CLI, update `llm-gemini` to 0.38 before evaluating Neon, and re-check pricing — the model mix and cost structure reportedly got more confusing, not less ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/18/llm-gemini/)).
2. If you're a Snowflake shop, you can already probe Gemini 4 Carbon/Hydrogen in governed Cortex workflows rather than bolting on a separate model gateway ([Snowflake release notes](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/all-release-notes)).
3. Before choosing between Astra, Qwen 4 Max, and Gemini 4 Neon, run your own evals on your own workloads — early third-party coverage is contradictory, with major benchmark claims alongside reports of unhappy testers ([AI Revolution](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_VXBCcECXJ4), [AI Search](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rYsIpPP6QY4)).

## Work Watch — Snowflake, dbt & Cortex Code

No dbt-specific release or modeling-pattern announcement made this 24-hour window, so the work-relevant content is Snowflake and Cortex Code only.

### Snowflake

- **New Cortex models in preview.** Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Hydrogen are now available in Public Preview in Snowflake Cortex AI ([release notes](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/all-release-notes)). This is the fastest way to test the new Gemini generation with SQL and governed data.
- **Prompt caching for Cortex AI Functions.** Snowflake introduced prompt caching on Nov 18 to cut repeated inference work ([release note](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2026/other/2026-11-18-cortex-prompt-caching)). The accompanying blog on AI function optimization shows how to use `AI_FUNCTION_EVALUATION` and `AI_FUNCTION_OPTIMIZATION` to hit quality goals at lower inference cost ([Snowflake blog](https://www.snowflake.com/content/snowflake-site/global/en/blog/ai-functions-optimization)). This is the cost-levers section worth reading this week.
- **Cortex AI Function Studio is GA.** Creating, evaluating, and optimizing Cortex AI Functions is now generally available ([release note](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2026/other/2026-11-16-cortex-ai-function-studio-ga)).
- **Cortex Agent SDK v1.0.0 is GA.** Python and TypeScript SDKs now support running Cortex Agents and Cortex Code, streaming responses, maintaining conversations/threads, and reconnecting to long-running work ([release note](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2026/other/2026-11-18-cortex-agent-sdk-ga)). This is the core building block if you want agents that operate inside Snowflake rather than calling out to an external orchestrator.
- **Retrieve Cortex Search is GA.** The managed retrieval endpoint moved to general availability ([release note](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2026/other/2026-11-18-cortex-search-retrieve-ga)).
- **Video AI is now first-class Cortex functionality.** Snowflake's Twelve Labs integration embeds, searches, and analyzes video with plain SQL; `AI_MULTI_EMBED` splits video into time-coded segments, each with vectors for what's on screen, all inside Snowflake's governance boundary ([Snowflake blog](https://www.snowflake.com/content/snowflake-site/global/en/blog/cortex-watch-hear-understand-video-twelvelabs), [demo video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oNRON2m_PSY)). If you've been ignoring unstructured video data, this now has a native SQL path.
- **Partner content worth a skim.** oneZero CEO Andrew Ralich published a Snowflake-partner piece on making trading data AI-ready, arguing LLM insights are only as powerful as the data quality behind them ([Medium](https://medium.com/snowflake/ai-is-only-as-powerful-as-the-data-behind-it-making-trading-data-ai-ready-9e17ab128829?source=rss----34b6daafc07---4)). Useful framing for data-platform work in financial services.

## Headlines

- **LLM-assisted reverse engineering is a hot discussion on Hacker News.** A new writeup on the subject has 270 points and 68 comments, suggesting real practitioner interest in using LLMs for binary/source analysis ([article](https://www.slowchronicle.com/posts/llm-assisted-reverse-engineering), [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50553225)). For agent work, this is a reminder that code understanding is becoming a practical LLM workload, not just a party trick.
- **OpenAI is marketing "dots" as proactive agent coworkers.** A short case study from Holiday Extras shows a director's dot Brian preparing a report during a morning commute, and a VP's dot Beast pulling performance numbers for a newly launched app feature before being asked ([OpenAI video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ikNf7eW8CHk)). This is the kind of agent loop worth studying for internal analytics: let agents proactively query product metrics instead of waiting for dashboards.
- **OpenAI also demoed ChatGPT for Accounting.** The new plugin finds completed jobs in email, drafts QuickBooks invoices for review, flags unclear items, reconciles transactions, processes invoices/receipts, and prepares monthly reports from spreadsheets and connected tools ([OpenAI video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AhmCKHNqhB8)). It's a practical example of AI agents entering finance workflows with human-in-the-loop review.
- **Agentic rewrites still need hard evidence.** Daniel Stenberg notes that while agents converted 185,000 lines of C into 340,000 lines of Rust for curl, the real curl project merged over 300 commits and 200 bugfixes during that same period — fixes the rewrites don't have ([quoted by Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/18/daniel-stenberg/)). The LOC explosion alone is a red flag; evaluate agentic refactors against upstream maintenance velocity, not just whether the code compiles.
- **Datasette is getting streaming export plugins.** Datasette-arrows 0.1a0 adds Arrow streaming exports, building on Datasette 1.0a43's new plugin API for streaming export formats ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/18/datasette-arrows/)). If you run Datasette as a lightweight analytics layer, this is the direction to watch.
