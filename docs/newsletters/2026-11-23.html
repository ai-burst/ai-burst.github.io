Breaking News

Reported: Claude helped certify a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis — a 167-year-old unsolved math problem — via a 17-million-line Lean proof. According to the AI Revolution roundup (u5aACewFWaw), Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says Claude "outpaced him at the finish line" of the proof, and the Lean and Mathlib communities have since certified it. Your AI Guy's coverage (lCe7tqNcLeE) is more cautious, framing the story as "reported" — the right default until a formal write-up circulates. The key detail is machine-checked: unlike most LLM math demos, this claim doesn't rest on a chatbot's confident prose but on a proof assistant's step-by-step verification, which is a much stronger category of evidence.

A second, related Claude result surfaced via Two Minute Papers (1jPdehG3Hms): an Anthropic/Hexagon Math paper on black-hole physics (anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics, hexagonmath.org/2611.00175v1). If the Riemann work were a one-off you could shrug; if Claude is producing a run of formally verified results, the "AI as research collaborator" story gets much more concrete — but stay skeptical of the "superintelligence is here" framing until the math community weighs in beyond the Lean certification.

Make the most of it:

  • Add formal-verification tasks to your evals. Lean/Mathlib-style formalization is one of the few reasoning benchmarks where the checker, not a judge or LLM-as-judge, decides correctness. Watch whether open-weight releases start reporting formal-math scores — that's a signal of real reasoning gains rather than benchmark cramming.
  • Borrow the draft-then-verify loop for agents. The reported workflow — model drafts, tool verifies, human closes the loop — maps directly onto coding agents with test harnesses: generate, verify, and spend your review budget where it matters.
  • Don't re-architect for one demo. Treat this as a strong signal, not a reason to bet the roadmap on AI mathematicians. Keep your eval harness model-agnostic so you can test the next model release locally on the same tasks.

Work Watch — Snowflake, dbt & Cortex Code

Snowflake

AI_COMPLETE now supports Snowflake Decision (public preview, Nov 20, 2026). Snowflake's release notes (1) add a new purpose-built model, Snowflake Decision, callable through AI_COMPLETE (6). It targets high-throughput classification, scoring, and decision-making across large datasets: evaluate unstructured data against predefined criteria and get structured answers with probabilities and confidence values — think ticket routing, record prioritization, or lead scoring without leaving SQL. That's the kind of feature that can replace brittle CASE WHEN heuristics, though it's worth benchmarking latency and cost per million rows against a small local model before committing any critical pipeline.

Snowflake app runtime: run Node.js and Python apps directly on Snowflake. A new Snowflake Developers walkthrough (rEzpwkQbzik) shows Snow App Deploy in the Snowflake CLI uploading and building your code, storing app versions, and returning a live URL. The use case that jumps out for a data/AI engineer: internal tools that currently sit in a tiny container somewhere can live inside Snowflake next to the data. Watch for platform lock-in and warehouse-compute billing, but the DX — CLI-driven, versioned deploys — looks genuinely convenient for shipping small internal apps.
